Renting out a granny flat on the Sunshine Coast

In short: A granny flat (secondary dwelling) can be rented long-term to anyone in Queensland, but the rental must meet smoke alarm and minimum housing standards, and renting can bring extra fire and sound requirements. Sunshine Coast Council treats short-term holiday letting differently: it is generally not a secondary dwelling use, may need planning approval, and attracts higher transitory accommodation rates.

If you're building a granny flat partly as an income stream, decide early whether it will be a long-term rental or a holiday let. The rules are very different. This page summarises official Queensland and Sunshine Coast Council sources. It is not legal, tax or planning advice.

Last updated: 27 November 2026

This page summarises Queensland Government, Residential Tenancies Authority (RTA) and Sunshine Coast Council information so you can plan. A building certifier, the RTA or council can confirm what applies to your property.

Long-term renting: allowed, with conditions

Since 26 September 2022, Queensland has allowed secondary dwellings to be rented to anyone, related to the main household or not. The Queensland Government says that if your existing granny flat didn't need planning approval, or its approval has no occupancy conditions, it can be rented out. If an older approval does restrict who can live there, you'd need to apply to change those conditions under the Planning Act 2016.

The change didn't alter building or fire rules. The Queensland Government says that when a home with a granny flat changes to a home with a rented granny flat, extra fire and sound transmission requirements of the building code apply, and this can trigger a building application. If you're building new with renting in mind, tell your builder and certifier at the start so separation and fire safety are designed in, not added later.

What the rental itself must meet

  • Smoke alarms: the RTA says all rental properties classed as a domestic dwelling, including secondary dwellings, must have interconnected photoelectric smoke alarms that comply with AS 3786-2014. The owner or manager must test them within 30 days before a tenancy starts or is renewed.
  • Minimum housing standards: every Queensland rental must meet them when the tenant moves in and throughout the tenancy. They include being weatherproof and structurally sound, working locks or latches on external doors and reachable windows, being free of vermin, damp and mould, window coverings for privacy in rooms such as bedrooms, hot and cold drinkable water, a private bathroom area with a flushing toilet, and a working cooktop if there's a kitchen.
  • Approvals first: the RTA says the owner must obtain all relevant development, building and council approvals before renting the secondary dwelling.

General tenancy or rooming accommodation?

The RTA says a self-contained granny flat, where the tenant has no access to the main house, is usually let under a general tenancy agreement. If the occupant shares a kitchen or other facilities, for example a granny flat with no kitchen whose tenant uses the main house's, or separate bedrooms let to different people, it's usually a rooming accommodation agreement. The agreement should say what part of the property is rented, how electricity, gas and water are paid, and any special terms such as parking, mail, bins, clothesline and internet.

Sunshine Coast rules that affect a rental

Sunshine Coast Council's secondary dwelling information sheet says a granny flat needs to share the main house's water connection and meter, wastewater connection, street number and letterbox, and driveway, and needs one extra on-site car park. Council's checklist uses these shared services as one of the signs that a second home is a secondary dwelling rather than a dual occupancy, which has different rules. Our QLD approvals guide covers the size and siting rules.

Holiday letting (Airbnb-style) is different

Council's information sheet says a secondary dwelling is for long-term residential use, meaning more than three consecutive months. If it's used for visitors or travellers, it's not a secondary dwelling under the Planning Regulation 2017 definition and is most likely short-term accommodation, and council asks you to contact it.

  • Planning: in the table of assessment for the Low density residential zone in the Sunshine Coast Planning Scheme 2014 that we checked, short-term accommodation isn't listed as its own use, so it falls under "all other uses", which are impact assessable. Ask council what applies to your lot and zone before you plan around holiday letting.
  • Rates: Sunshine Coast Council's 2026–27 Revenue Statement has separate Transitory Accommodation rating categories for properties offered, available or used for rental in a temporary manner, typically for periods under 42 consecutive days. For the lowest-value band (category 16T), the 2026–27 minimum general rate is $4,253. Council's statement lists exclusions, such as a property with a documented tenancy of 42 consecutive days or more under the residential tenancies law.

Questions to ask before you build

  • Will the design meet the fire separation and sound transmission rules for a rented granny flat?
  • Are compliant interconnected photoelectric smoke alarms included in the price?
  • Will it share the main house's water meter, sewer connection and driveway, as council's sheet describes?
  • Is there room for the extra car park?
  • Does my zone or any overlay change what's allowed? (Ask the certifier to check council's mapping.)
