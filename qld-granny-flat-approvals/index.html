QLD granny flat approvals on the Sunshine Coast

In short: In Queensland, every new granny flat (secondary dwelling) needs building approval, and some also need development approval from council. Since 26 September 2022, a secondary dwelling can be rented to anyone.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal or planning advice.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This page summarises Queensland Government and Sunshine Coast Council information so you know what to expect. It is not legal or planning advice. Your builder, a building certifier or council can confirm what applies to your lot.

Building approval is always needed

The Queensland Government says any new secondary dwelling (granny flat) requires building approval, and you should speak with your council to find out whether you also need development approval. The same applies to converting an existing structure into a granny flat.

Renting a granny flat

Since 26 September 2022, Queensland has allowed secondary dwellings to be rented to anyone, whether or not they are related to the household in the main house. Renting doesn't remove other requirements: when a granny flat is rented out, extra fire safety and sound transmission requirements of the building code can apply, so get advice from a building certifier.

Source: Queensland Government, Secondary dwellings providing housing solutions.

Sunshine Coast Council

Sunshine Coast Council's secondary dwelling information sheet (for the Sunshine Coast Planning Scheme 2014) says a new secondary dwelling is generally accepted development, with no planning approval needed, in zones such as Low density residential when it meets the Planning Regulation criteria. Under the Dwelling house code, a secondary dwelling should:

  • be within 20 metres of the main house if it is freestanding
  • share services and access with the main house (water connection and meter, wastewater, street number and letterbox, driveway)
  • have a gross floor area of no more than 60 m² (90 m² in the Rural and Rural residential zones, or 45 m² and no more than 4 m high in the Moffat Beach, Shelly Beach and Dicky Beach area)
  • have one extra on-site car park
  • in an urban zone, be on a regular-shaped lot of at least 600 m²

All secondary dwellings need building approval from a private building certifier, and council issues the plumbing and drainage permit. Overlays, setbacks and estate plans can add requirements, so check your property in council's mapping or with a certifier. The planning scheme can be updated, so check the current version with council.

Source: Sunshine Coast Council, Secondary dwellings information sheet.

Licences, contracts and insurance

In Queensland, the QBCC says domestic building work over $3,300 needs a written contract, and the contractor must arrange Queensland Home Warranty Scheme cover. The deposit before work starts can be no more than 10% for contracts from $3,301 to $19,999, or 5% for contracts of $20,000 or more. Contracts over $3,300 also come with a cooling-off period (generally five business days). Check the contractor's QBCC licence before you sign.

Sources: QBCC, Domestic building contracts; QBCC, Deposits and progress payments; QBCC, Cooling-off period.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources or ask a certifier before relying on this summary.
